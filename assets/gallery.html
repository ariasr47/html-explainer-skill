How an explainer is built

Reading time about 7 minutesVersion 1.0, 2026-10-07For: anyone writing or reading one

An explainer is a page you can skim in a minute and still get the point: the answer comes first, colour has one meaning, every chapter has a picture, and the page remembers so you do not have to.

Keep in your head (only these)
  1. Yellow means "this is what you keep".
  2. Every chapter stands on its own.
  3. Skim mode shows the story in pictures; one-chapter mode shows one thing at a time.

Chapter 1 of 7, about 2 minutes

Your working memory is the limit, so the page carries the load

Before this: the answer at the top said the page remembers things so you do not have to. This chapter shows how.

Reading a long document means holding earlier facts while you take in new ones. With a short working memory, the earlier facts fall out as you scroll. Then you scroll back, lose your place, and give up.

  1. Read a factSection one says X.
  2. ScrollThree screens of new stuff.
  3. Forget XSection four needs it.
  4. Scroll backLose your place. Stop.

An explainer breaks that loop by restating context exactly where it is needed. Four devices do this, and they are all the same yellow so you learn them once.

  1. Keep boxThree facts, at the top.
  2. Before thisEach chapter restates its one input.
  3. Remember lineEach chapter ends with one sentence.
  4. RecapThe remember lines, collected.
🗒️

It is like reading with sticky notes already on the page. Someone stuck a note on every section saying what you need from earlier. You never flip back. Where it stops: here the notes are not optional.

Why three, and why yellow

Working memory research puts the practical span at three to four items for most adults, lower under stress or with ADHD. Three is the safe number. One colour for all memory devices means one association to learn instead of four.

Show the reading controls
  • Read aloud reads one chapter, one block at a time. It appears when your browser can speak.
  • Reading ruler keeps the block under your pointer clear and fades the rest of the chapter.
  • Text size steps through normal, large and extra large.
  • Keys lists the shortcuts: J and K change chapter, F, S, D and R switch the modes.
  • When you come back, the page offers to continue where you stopped. When every chapter is done, it says so.

Remember: anything yellow is something the page is holding for you; you can stop holding it.

Chapter 2 of 7, about 2 minutes

Colour means one thing, and never decorates

Before this: yellow is the page holding something for you. Five more colours carry meaning the same way.

Attention goes to the brightest thing on screen. If bright colour is spent on decoration, attention is spent on nothing. So the page is calm fog and navy, and colour appears only when it says something.

  • GreenDone, safe, yes, recommended.
  • AmberIn progress, caution, needs a check before you rely on it.
  • RedBlocked, risk, no.
  • PurpleYou must choose. The page cannot go on without you.
  • CobaltYou are here: the answer bar, the current chapter, links, the current step.
  • YellowHeld for you: keep box, remember lines, recap.

Each dot also carries a symbol, so colour never works alone.

Callouts use the same three meaning colours, and only when they change what you should do next.

Tip

A tip is something that makes the next step easier. If it does not change what you do, it is not a tip and it is not on the page.

Check before relying on this

Amber marks a thing that is probably fine but has not been verified. The page says what would verify it.

Do not do this

Red marks a step that breaks something. It says what breaks and what to do instead.

Remember: if a colour shows up, it is telling you something; if nothing is coloured, nothing needs you.

Chapter 3 of 7, about 1 minute

Show the relationship instead of describing it

Before this: colour is used only for meaning. Diagrams use the same six colours, and the same rule.

A paragraph that describes a branch makes you hold both branches in your head. A diagram puts them on the screen side by side. That is the whole reason to draw one.

Most relationships do not need a drawn diagram. A sequence is a flow. Two states of one thing are a split.

Before: a paragraph
  • "After validation the request is either rejected with a message or forwarded, unless…"
  • Reader holds two paths.
  • Reader rereads.
After: the diagram above
  • Both paths visible.
  • One caption states the point.
  • Paragraph deleted.
The rules for a drawn diagram
  • Seven boxes at most. Left to right, then top to bottom.
  • Labels under four words. Details in the caption.
  • One "you are here" box at most. No crossing lines.
  • Colour classes, never hard-coded fills, so dark mode works.
  • A caption that says the one thing to take from it. No caption, no diagram.

Remember: when you see a diagram, read its caption first; the caption is the point, the drawing is the proof.

Chapter 4 of 7, about 2 minutes

When you must decide, the page lays the choice out for you

Before this: purple means "you must choose". This chapter shows what a purple card contains.

A decision buried in prose costs a reread per option. A decision card puts the options side by side, marks the one the writer recommends, and says what happens if you decide nothing. You can choose in a minute.

Should the explainer load web fonts?

The page must read well offline. Fonts change how it reads online.

Load them, fall back quietly

  • Best reading experience online
  • Offline still reads well on system fonts
  • One request to Google Fonts

Cost: none to the reader.

System fonts only

  • Zero network
  • Reading-optimised faces lost
  • Looks different on every machine

Cost: a worse read for most people.

Embed the font files

  • Identical everywhere
  • Adds about 300 KB per page
  • Licence bookkeeping

Cost: every explainer gets heavy.

If you do nothing: the template already loads them with a fallback. Nothing breaks.

When the decision leads to action, the actions are a ladder. Each rung is one verb and one line.

  1. Read the answer bar. If you agree, you can stop here.
  2. Open the decision card. Compare the option columns.
  3. Pick. Use the buttons in the ask at the end of the page.

Remember: a purple card always tells you the recommended option and what happens if you ignore it.

Chapter 6 of 7, about 2 minutes

Plain words on the path, machinery in the folds

Before this: every device so far reduces what you must hold. Words are the last place that load hides.

The reading path is written the way you would say it out loud: short sentences, one idea each, the verb near the front. A technical word gets a plain definition the first time it appears, like idempotent below.

Idempotent
Safe to run twice. The second run changes nothing.
Reading path
Everything you see with all the folds closed.
Fold
A closed box you can open for exact details.

Everything exact lives in a fold: commands, file paths, hashes, tables. The page must make sense with every fold closed, so opening one is always optional.

One fact, at three depths.

The page remembers what you typed.

Each answer is stored in this browser, under the page address.

localStorage["explainer:/page:answers"]

Show the checker command 
node <plugin folder>/scripts/check.mjs docs/explainers/2026-10-07-topic.html

Exit code 0 means every hard rule passed. It fails a page for a missing answer bar, a chapter without a visual, a paragraph over forty words, an external script, or an SVG without a title.

Before
  • "Session tokens expire after 3600 s and are subsequently invalidated server-side."
After
  • "The server forgets who you are after an hour."
  • Exact number in a fold.

Remember: if you hit a hard word or a wall of detail in the reading path, the page is wrong, not you.

Chapter 7 of 7, about 1 minute

Questions get answered on the page, and the answers stay with you

Before this: purple means you must choose. A question field is where you do it.

Some pages ask you things. Answer, and the page saves it on this device. A bar at the bottom then offers to copy or download.

One sentence is enough.

Show the markup for the other kinds

The default kind is text. Set data-kind for the rest. Every field needs a stable data-q.

Chapter 1 of 6, about 1 minute

Does 3D or animation help a reader understand?

Before this: you asked whether WebGL, Three.js and other rich visual libraries could take explainers to the next level.

The best test found compared the same data stories as static, animated and 3D pages. The 3D version won on interest and lost on understanding.

600people compared static, animated and 3D versions
0comprehension gain for 3D; static and animated rated easier
90%of build time went to the 3D version
  • 3D immersionMore interesting, harder to follow. 11 of 40 comments called the movement distracting, 7 called flashy visuals untrustworthy.
  • Animation for novicesNovices learned more from static diagrams than from animation; only experienced readers did as well or better with motion.
  • Animation with a jobMotion earns its place for state changes, causality and uncertainty. Students watching maths animations learned, but overrated how much the motion helped.
  • ScrollytellingIn 454 people it matched plain text and labels on comprehension. No advantage.

So the ladder stays mostly where it is. Climb only when the subject itself is spatial.

  1. CSS componentsSequence, compare, status, choice.
  2. Inline SVGBranches, maps, wireframes.
  3. Steps, no libraryOne picture, several states.
  4. Small librariesReal data, scrubbable motion. Opt-in.
  5. 3D viewerPhysical objects only. Behind a button.
Show the sources
  • Kim et al. 2024, "Understanding the Impact of Spatial Immersion in Web Data Stories", 600 Prolific participants, within-subjects. Interest up in 3 of 4 stories; no per-condition recall reported.
  • Kalyuga 2007, "Expertise Reversal Effect", Educational Psychology Review. Static diagrams beat animation for novices.
  • Bos and Wigmans 2025, Utrecht, 3Blue1Brown videos with about 20 calculus students per session, pre and post tests.
  • Mendez and Such 2026, scrollytelling versus text and labels, N = 454, between-subjects.
  • Hohman, Conlen, Heer and Chau, Distill 2020. Animation's demonstrated value limited to state transitions, uncertainty, causality, narrative.
  • Library sizes measured today from jsDelivr: Three.js 675 KB, model-viewer 933 KB, Babylon 4.9 MB, Mermaid 2.5 MB, GSAP 70 KB, Rough.js 27 KB.

Remember: 3D and motion are rewards, not lessons; the system allows them only behind a button and only for physical things.

Chapter 2 of 6, about 1 minute

Why guided interaction wins and free exploration loses

Before this: rich motion and 3D do not improve understanding. Interactivity is the next candidate, and the evidence splits it in two.

A meta-analysis of 164 studies settled the question for novices. Letting people explore on their own loses to being shown. Leading them through it with feedback wins.

Free exploration
  • Loses to direct instruction (d = -0.38).
  • Loses to worked examples by d = -0.63.
  • Takes longer and feels harder.
Guided steps with feedback
  • Beats other instruction (d = +0.30).
  • Fixed segments: retention d = 0.42.
  • ADHD novices: 87% fewer errors with single-step segments and 4-second pauses.

One more hard lesson comes from the newsroom that invented the modern explainer. Most readers never click.

Never hide the lesson behind a click

The New York Times graphics desk found readers skip steppers, tabs and sliders, and never see tooltips. The default state of every interactive figure must already make the point. Steps add depth, they never carry the message.

  1. Default viewAlready shows the conclusion.
  2. Fixed stepsNext and Previous, one change each.
  3. PresetsThree worked cases to tap.
  4. Free sliderLast, and only after presets.
Show the sources
  • Alfieri, Brooks, Aldrich and Tenenbaum 2011, Journal of Educational Psychology, 164 studies. Unassisted discovery d = -0.38 (580 comparisons); enhanced discovery d = +0.30 (360 comparisons); time-to-answer favoured non-discovery (d = -0.72).
  • Rey et al. 2019, meta-analysis of the segmenting effect, 56 investigations. Retention d = 0.32, system-paced segments d = 0.42; learner-paced effects less confirmed.
  • Pimenova et al. 2026, ASSETS, 17 programmers with ADHD and 10 without. Single-instruction segments with 4-second pauses cut errors by about 87%; the ADHD-specific extra was a trend, not significant.
  • Archie Tse, NYT, "Why we are doing fewer interactives", Malofiej 2016.
  • Kalyuga 2007: learner control not reliably beneficial; embedded labels on diagrams help novices (effect sizes above 1.6) and hurt experts.

Remember: the default view shows the point, steps add depth, presets come before sliders, and nothing essential ever hides behind a click.

Chapter 3 of 6, about 1 minute

Which cheap reading aids actually have evidence?

Before this: interaction helps only when it is guided. The features below need no library and no 3D, and most of them have better evidence than either.

Two of these were tested on readers like you. Read-aloud raised comprehension in the ADHD group and did nothing for typical readers. A reading ruler sped up everyone.

  • Read aloudComprehension up for ADHD readers with reading difficulty; dyslexic readers got speed, not comprehension; typical readers were disrupted. Built into every browser, zero dependencies.
  • Reading rulerGrey bar, shade or underline styles added 10 to 20 words a minute for 177 readers, half dyslexic.
  • Fixed pauses between stepsThe 87% error cut above. Four seconds was the sweet spot; two felt rushed, six lost people.
  • Time per chapterADHD shows medium deficits in judging time across 55 studies. A stated cost lowers the barrier to starting.
  • Completion feedbackGamified tasks were more engaging in every study and ADHD children completed more trials, but performance was mixed. Keep it light.
  • Letter and word spacing controlsLetterforms did nothing; extra letter spacing alone slowed reading; effects were tiny. Offer it, expect little.
Show the sources
  • Wood et al. 2018, Journal of Learning Disabilities, 22 studies, 2,942 readers: text-to-speech d = .35 (CI .14 to .56); between-subject designs d = .61. Read-along highlighting was never tested.
  • Chen, Hung and Jian 2026, Reading and Writing, eye-tracking, junior high students: text-to-speech improved comprehension in the ADHD-related group, not in typical readers.
  • Niklaus, Cai, Bylinskii and Wallace 2023, CHI, "Digital Reading Rulers", 177 crowdsourced readers.
  • Marx, Cortese, Koelch and Hacker 2022, JAACAP meta-analysis of timing in ADHD, 55 studies.
  • Lumsden et al. 2016, JMIR Serious Games, systematic review of gamification, 33 studies, six in ADHD.
  • Galliussi et al. 2020, Annals of Dyslexia, 128 Italian children: letterform and spacing effects.

Remember: read-aloud, a reading ruler, fixed pauses and a time per chapter are the evidence-backed upgrades; spacing controls and games are extras.

Chapter 4 of 6, about 1 minute

What is wrong with the current page, measured

Before this: the evidence points at cheap features, not big libraries. Before adding any, the page has two defects an audit found today.

An accessibility audit ran on the gallery page in both themes. Body text, links and the yellow memory boxes pass. Two groups fail.

2.0 to 2.5dark-mode contrast of white text on coloured buttons and badges; 4.5 is the minimum
3.2light-mode contrast of the small grey kicker text
5.6 to 14.6everything else, both themes
  • Dark-mode fillsThe meaning colours are lightened for dark backgrounds, but the text on them stays white. Fix: "on-colour" text tokens that flip to dark ink in dark mode.
  • Kicker greyToo faint in light mode. Fix: darken the tertiary ink token one step.
  • Colour-only dotsStatus and timeline dots carry meaning by colour alone, which fails the lowest WCAG level. Fix: a glyph in every dot and step number.
  • CheckerTeach it to compute contrast from the tokens so this cannot come back.
Show the measurements

axe-core 4.10.2 in the browser, light and dark. Light: 7 violations, all .kicker at 3.23 to 3.54 against 4.5. Dark: white on --here 2.47, on --go 1.97, on --decide 2.30, on --ink-soft 2.07. Amber text on white in light mode is 3.99, fine only at large sizes. Full table in docs/research/2026-10-07-next-level.md.

Remember: dark-mode buttons and light kickers fail contrast and the dots are colour-only; all three are token fixes, about an hour.

Chapter 5 of 6, about 1 minute

Your brother's use: pages that ask and collect

Before this: the fixes above keep the page honest. This chapter is about a new job the page is already doing in the wild.

Your brother explains a topic to coworkers, adds text fields under his questions, and copies every answer into one text. That is requirement gathering, and the system should support it on purpose.

  1. ExplainThe chapter, as today.
  2. AskOne question, one field, says what a good answer holds.
  3. TypeSaved in the browser, nothing leaves the page.
  4. Copy allFixed shape: question verbatim, then answer.
  5. MergePaste several people's copies into one view.
  1. Answer field. A labelled text box or fixed choices under any question, with an "answered" tick that also lights the rail dot.
  2. Collect bar. Appears after the first answer: "3 of 5 answered, copy all answers". Also download as Markdown or copy as JSON.
  3. Merge tool. For the author: paste everyone's copied text, get one view per question with names.
Why this fits the evidence

Typing an answer is a self-explanation prompt, one of the few interactive mechanisms the literature ties to comprehension. The field is the lesson's worked example turned around.

Remember: explain, ask, type, copy all, merge; answer fields become a page type with their own checker rules.

Chapter 6 of 6, about 1 minute

Five phases, and which one is worth starting

Before this: the evidence favours cheap guided features and fixes over big libraries. The roadmap puts them in that order.

  1. Phase 0, 1 hourContrast tokens, glyphs in dots, gallery synced with your skim fix.
  2. Phase 1, about a dayAnswer fields and collect bar, stepper figure, perspectives tabs, read aloud, reading ruler, resume, time per chapter.
  3. Phase 2, 1 to 2 daysDiagram compiler, inliner, checker v2 with contrast, fidelity gate, five evals, example pages.
  4. Phase 3, 1 to 2 daysScreenshot frame, isometric stack, presets then sliders, 3D viewer behind a button.
  5. Phase 4, half a dayAsk-this-page when published online; plugin release through your catalog.
